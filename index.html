The research question

Market adjustment begins with a measurement problem.

A delayed price response can reflect gradual learning, a stale quote, an incorrectly aligned clock or the arrival of further news. The study asks which interpretations remain defensible after checking those possibilities.

As Hasbrouck (2007) explains in Empirical market microstructure, the observation process matters when interpreting prices. Here, a source-clock audit becomes a central part of the economic analysis.

01

Establish the information clock

Align scheduled FOMC statements and minutes with provider quotes; audit missing observations and seasonal timestamp behaviour.

02

Estimate the response path

Hold the development rate-surprise factor fixed and estimate each completed minute separately, with uncertainty across horizons.

03

Test what the model can support

Use six simulated mechanisms, matched-day comparisons and chronological prediction checks before interpreting an adjustment speed.

Main findings / Accepted SPX statement windows

An early response. An unresolved adjustment speed.

The statement association appears early. Meeting minutes show a more delayed exploratory pattern. Neither communication type meets every criterion for a reliable single time-to-adjustment estimate.

Prediction checks show no convincing improvement over a development-mean baseline. The rate factor uses information through minute +20; earlier response estimates are retrospective associations.
